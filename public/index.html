Why it exists

The expensive part often starts before the expensive review.

Teams spend time collecting public signals, reading supplied documents, spotting missing evidence and writing the same follow-up questions. EvidenceCheck makes that first pass repeatable and easier to hand off.

What do we actually know?

Not “does this vendor look good?” Instead: which signals are observable, which claims lack evidence, and what should be verified next?

Trust boundary

Useful because the limits are explicit.

E

Evidence ≠ proof

Public signals can inform a first pass; they do not establish certification, control effectiveness or absence of vulnerabilities.

H

Human accountability

Material procurement, security and compliance decisions remain with the responsible team.

B

Bounded scope

EvidenceCheck is not a penetration test, legal opinion, certification audit or replacement for authorized review.

FAQ

Common questions

What makes this different from a generic AI summary?

The output is organized around observable signals, missing evidence, verification questions and limitations. The goal is a repeatable review queue, not a persuasive paragraph.

Can an AI agent buy it directly?

Yes. The machine endpoints expose discovery metadata and x402 payment terms for compatible agent workflows. See the agent page and OpenAPI specification.

Do I need a subscription?

No subscription is required for the machine endpoints. They are priced per request in USDC on Base.

Does a result certify a vendor?

No. Results are first-pass evidence and gaps. They should be verified by the responsible buyer or security/compliance team.
